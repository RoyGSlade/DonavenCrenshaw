Source Arcanum is a local-first umbrella built around one stubborn idea: **software should be something you own** - not something you rent, petition, or re-earn every month.

This is the trust anchor. The rulebook. The line in the sand.

## The Seven Rules (Non-Negotiables)

1. **Ownership by default.** Tools should keep working offline. If it cannot run without the internet, you do not truly own it.
2. **Free forever.** Zero cost to use. No subscriptions. No feature gating. No "free until we pivot."
3. **No ads. No tracking. No telemetry.** If a product needs surveillance to survive, it should not exist.
4. **Accounts must be local.** If an account exists at all, it should live on *your* machine, remain private, and be hardened to minimize leak risk.
5. **Security is a feature, not a checkbox.** Sometimes you may self-host, use a VM, or use a VPN for defense-in-depth - but the goal is to build systems strong enough that external subscription "security crutches" are not required.
6. **Empower the end user.** Tools should simplify the hard parts and return agency to the person using them, not the company selling them.
7. **Open source by default (MIT).** Everything released is readable, forkable, and modifiable - so users can verify behavior, fix issues, or continue development without waiting on permission.

## The Enemy (What This Rejects)

"Rental software" is the shape of the trap: programs that mostly run on your computer, but demand a monthly toll forever. It is not just annoying - it is a slow conversion of tools into leashes.

Run the boring math: a tool that costs **$40/month** costs about **$480/year**. Over 5-10 years, you are looking at **thousands of dollars** for something that often never truly becomes yours. For non-commercial users who just need to do one thing quickly, that is absurd.

The other enemy is more subtle: **surveillance + AI correlation**. When a platform can tie identity, behavior, images, relationships, and purchases together, the user becomes a dataset. That dataset gets sold - to advertisers, to influence machines, sometimes to governments, and sometimes to whoever asks nicely with enough money.

Convenience becomes a tax on independent thought. Source Arcanum exists to offer an exit ramp.

## Trust Architecture (How You Can Verify This)

I am not asking for blind trust. I am trying to build systems where trust is *earned* by structure:

- **Open repositories** with readable code (MIT).
- **No secret API dependencies** baked into the core experience.
- **Static GitHub Pages site** as a transparent hub: projects, chronicles, and release notes.
- **Minimal data collection** as a principle: the default is "store locally, store less."

Important boundary: I will not publish API keys or private credentials. Everything else is meant to be auditable.

## Donations as Votes (Status: Coming Soon)

The donation-steered roadmap is currently **not fully live** - right now it is partially hooked up and evolving. The goal is simple: donations steer *priority*, not access.

Current support rails are **Ko-fi** and **GitHub Sponsors**. I do not love their privacy posture, but payments on the modern internet are effectively monopolized by a small set of processors. For now, third-party rails are a necessary compromise.

The long-term plan is to build a more Source-Arcanum-aligned support system that captures only:

- **amount**
- **what you are voting to prioritize**

And nothing else. No accounts. No profiling. No weird loyalty tier economy.
